# ACC1701X

This hub page provides an overview of **ACC1701X: Accounting for Decision Makers** (Y1S1), a module designed to equip students with the fundamentals of financial accounting for informed decision-making.

## Module Overview

ACC1701X is taught by Dr. Hanny Kusnadi, with tutorials led by Ms. Elaine Tan, Ms. Yong Ching Lian, and Mr. Phillip Toh [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-01-slides-accounting-in-business-and-fs-overview-pdf#section-1]. The required textbook is "Financial Accounting: IFRS Edition", 3rd Edition by Stice et al. (Cengage Learning Asia Pte Ltd) [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-01-slides-accounting-in-business-and-fs-overview-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-textbook-pdf#page-1]. The module emphasizes understanding accounting as the "language of business" to analyze financial news and evaluate company fundamentals [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-01-post-lecture-supplement-pdf#section-1].

## Assessment

The module's assessment comprises:
*   Class Participation: 20% [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-01-slides-accounting-in-business-and-fs-overview-pdf#section-1]
*   Midterm Test: 30% [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-01-slides-accounting-in-business-and-fs-overview-pdf#section-1]
*   Final Examination: 50% [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-01-slides-accounting-in-business-and-fs-overview-pdf#section-1]

The final exam is a 2-hour, closed-book, on-site assessment that requires university-approved calculators. It covers content from Chapters 1-10, 12, and 13, including both multiple-choice and problem-solving questions [source:acc1701x-finals-acc1701-review-packet-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-finals-final-exam-announcement-mar-23-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-finals-ay17-18-acc1701-ay17-18-sem-1-finals-pdf#section-1].

## Key Concepts and Topics

Throughout the module, students explore fundamental accounting principles and their application:

*   **The Accounting Cycle**: This foundational process includes analyzing transactions, preparing journal entries, posting to T-accounts, generating trial balances (unadjusted, adjusted, and post-closing), making adjusting entries, preparing financial statements, and performing closing entries [source:acc1701x-tutorial-halocrypto-tutorial-4-completing-the-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-lecture-lecture-02-slides-mechanics-of-accounting-pdf#section-1].
*   **Financial Statements**: Understanding the core financial statements is central, including the Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet), Statement of Profit & Loss (Income Statement), Statement of Changes in Equity, and Statement of Cash Flows [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-02-slides-mechanics-of-accounting-pdf#section-1].
*   **Adjusting Accounts**: This topic covers accrual-basis accounting, emphasizing the time period assumption, revenue recognition, and matching principles. It involves preparing adjusting entries for unearned revenues, accrued revenues, prepaid expenses, and accrued expenses. Cash is typically excluded from adjusting entries [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-03-slides-adjusting-accounts-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-1].
*   **Financial Statement Integrity & Internal Controls**: This area addresses potential issues like errors, disagreements in judgments, and fraud. It introduces principles of internal controls—such as establishing responsibilities, segregating duties, and maintaining adequate records—to ensure the reliability of financial reporting [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-05-slides-fs-integrity-pdf#section-1].
*   **Receivables**: Topics include accounting for accounts receivable (trade receivables) and notes receivable, the allowance method for estimating uncollectible accounts, and the concept of expected credit loss [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-06-slides-receivables-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-tutorial-samsung-tutorial-5-recap-receivables-amended-pdf#section-1].
*   **Cash & Current Liabilities**: This section covers cash management, internal controls over cash, bank reconciliation, and accounting for current liabilities such as sales tax payable, payroll liabilities, short-term notes payable, and estimated liabilities like warranty provisions [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-07-slides-cash-and-current-liabilities-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-tutorial-samsung-tutorial-6-recap-cash-current-liabilities-pdf#section-1].
*   **Inventory & Cost of Sales**: Students learn about inventory systems (perpetual vs. periodic), costing methods (FIFO, LIFO, weighted average), the lower of cost or net realizable value rule, and the impact of inventory errors on financial statements [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-08-slides-inventory-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-lecture-tutorial-7-recap-inventory-pdf#section-1].
*   **Property, Plant & Equipment (PPE) & Intangible Assets**: This covers the acquisition, capitalization, depreciation (straight-line, units-of-production, declining-balance), impairment, and disposal of tangible assets, as well as the amortization of intangible assets [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-09-ppe-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-tutorial-samsung-tutorial-8-recap-ppe-pdf#section-1].
*   **Equity**: Key concepts include different types of shares (ordinary/common, preferred), authorized, issued, outstanding, and treasury shares, as well as the accounting for dividends (cash and stock) and share splits [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-10-slides-equity-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-tutorial-samsung-tutorial-9-recap-equity-pdf#section-1].
*   **Statement of Cash Flows (SCF)**: The SCF highlights cash inflows and outflows across operating, investing, and financing activities. The module focuses on preparing the SCF using the indirect method for operating activities; the direct method is not covered or examined [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-11-slides-statement-of-cash-flows-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-lecture-lecture-11-post-lecture-supplement-pdf#section-1].
*   **Financial Statement Analysis (FSA)**: This involves using techniques like horizontal (trend) analysis, vertical (common-size percent) analysis, and ratio analysis (e.g., profitability, liquidity, solvency, efficiency ratios) to evaluate a company's financial performance. The DuPont Framework, which decomposes Return on Equity (ROE) into profitability, efficiency, and leverage components, is also explored [source:acc1701x-lecture-lecture-12-slides-analyzing-fs-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-12-supplement-pdf#section-1, acc1701x-tutorial-samsung-tutorial-11-recap-financial-statement-analysis-pdf#section-1].
