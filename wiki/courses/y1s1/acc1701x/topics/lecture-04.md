# Lecture 04: Completing Accounting Cycle

This page covers the completion of the accounting cycle, focusing on the steps following adjusting entries, including the preparation of financial statements, the closing process, and financial analysis using the profit margin ratio. It emphasizes the importance of accrual accounting and periodic reporting.

## Prior Lecture Refresher

From previous lectures, it's important to recall the following core concepts [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-1]:
*   **Accrual Basis of Accounting:** This method ensures a better matching of revenues and expenses.
    *   **Revenue Recognition Principle:** Revenues are recognized when earned, regardless of when cash is received.
    *   **Matching Principle (Expense Recognition):** Expenses are recognized when incurred to generate revenue, regardless of when cash is paid.
*   **Adjustments:** These entries are crucial for allocating revenues and expenses to the correct accounting period. Common types of adjustments include:
    *   Unearned Revenue / Accrued Revenue
    *   Prepaid Expense / Accrued Expense

    *   Closing the books.

## The Accounting Cycle

The accounting cycle is a series of steps that businesses follow to record, summarize, and report financial transactions. Today's focus covers the final stages of this cycle [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-5]:
*   Preparing the [[adjusted-trial-balance|Adjusted Trial Balance]]
*   Preparing Financial Statements
*   Preparing Closing Journal Entries & Posting to Accounts
*   Preparing the Post-Closing Trial Balance

All public companies use the **accrual basis of accounting** [source: acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-10].

## The Adjusted Trial Balance

Recall that in [[lecture-03]] we learned about adjustments (Adjusting Journal Entries or AJEs). After AJEs are recorded and posted, they update the unadjusted trial balance, leading to the creation of an **adjusted trial balance** [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-7].

*   An **unadjusted trial balance** is a list of accounts and their balances prepared *before* adjustments are recorded [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-7].
*   An **adjusted trial balance** is a list of accounts and their balances prepared *after* adjusting entries have been recorded and posted to the ledger [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-7].

**Key consideration for adjusting entries:** Cash is the account least likely to appear in an adjusting journal entry [source: acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-1].

### Minion Co. Example: Recording Adjusting Journal Entries (AJEs)

Let's review how adjusting entries modify the unadjusted trial balance, using Minion Co. at the end of 2022 as an example [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-8].

**Original Unadjusted Trial Balance (Partial)** [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-9]
| Account Types | Debit | Credit |
| :---------------------- | :---- | :----- |
| Unearned Revenue | | $19,500 |
| Sales Revenue | | $65,000 |
| Cost of Sales | $15,000 | |
| Inventory | $20,000 | |
| Prepaid Expenses | $14,000 | |
| Rent Expense | | |
| Accumulated Depreciation | | $20,000 |
| Depreciation Expense | | |
| Allowance for Bad Debts | | |
| Bad Debt Expense | | |
| Income Tax Payable | | $1,500 |
| Income Tax Expense | | |

**A. Unearned Revenue Adjustment**
Minion Co. delivered $8,500 worth of goods in 2022, for which advance payment was received in 2021 [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-9].
*   **Adjustment:** This reduces the liability (Unearned Revenue) and recognizes the revenue earned. When an adjustment reduces unearned revenue and recognizes sales revenue, two accounts are involved in the adjusting journal entry [source: acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-2].
    *   Debit: Unearned Revenue $8,500
    *   Credit: Sales Revenue $8,500
*   **Impact on Trial Balance:**
    *   Unearned Revenue: $19,500 (Credit) - $8,500 (Debit) = $11,000 (Credit)
    *   Sales Revenue: $65,000 (Credit) + $8,500 (Credit) = $73,500 (Credit)

**B. Cost of Goods Sold/Sales Adjustment**
The cost of sales related to the goods delivered in event (a) is $1,700 [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-9].
*   **Adjustment:**
    *   Debit: Cost of Sales $1,700
    *   Credit: Inventory $1,700
*   **Impact on Trial Balance:**
    *   Cost of Sales: $15,000 (Debit) + $1,700 (Debit) = $16,700 (Debit)
    *   Inventory: $20,000 (Debit) - $1,700 (Credit) = $18,300 (Debit)

**C. Prepaid Expense Adjustment**
Minion Co. paid rent in advance in 2020. The rent allocation for 2022 is $7,000 [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-10].
*   **Adjustment:** This recognizes the portion of prepaid rent that has been used up as an expense.
    *   Debit: Rent Expense $7,000
    *   Credit: Prepaid Expenses (Rent) $7,000
*   **Impact on Trial Balance:**
    *   Prepaid Expenses: $14,000 (Debit) - $7,000 (Credit) = $7,000 (Debit)
    *   Rent Expense: $7,000 (Debit)

**D. Depreciation Expense Adjustment**
In 2020, Minion purchased Property, Plant & Equipment (PP&E) for $100,000. The annual depreciation expense is $10,000 for 10 years [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-10].
*   **Adjustment:** This recognizes the expense of using up PP&E over time.
    *   Debit: Depreciation Expense $10,000
    *   Credit: Accumulated Depreciation $10,000
*   **Impact on Trial Balance:**
    *   Accumulated Depreciation: $20,000 (Credit) + $10,000 (Credit) = $30,000 (Credit)
    *   Depreciation Expense: $10,000 (Debit)

**E. Bad Debt Expense Adjustment**
Based on past experience, Minion estimates bad debt expense for 2022 to be 4% of $45,000 in Accounts Receivable, totaling $1,800. The contra-asset account to Receivables is Allowance for Bad Debts [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-11]. A contra-asset account typically has a normal credit balance.
*   **Adjustment:** This recognizes the estimated portion of receivables that will not be collected.
    *   Debit: Bad Debt Expense $1,800
    *   Credit: Allowance for Bad Debts $1,800
*   **Impact on Trial Balance:**
    *   Allowance for Bad Debts: $1,800 (Credit)
    *   Bad Debt Expense: $1,800 (Debit)

**F. Income Tax Expense Adjustment**
Minion estimates income tax in 2022 to be $1,000 [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-11].
*   **Adjustment:** This recognizes the income tax liability for the period.
    *   Debit: Income Tax Expense $1,000
    *   Credit: Income Tax Payable $1,000
*   **Impact on Trial Balance:**
    *   Income Tax Payable: $1,500 (Credit) + $1,000 (Credit) = $2,500 (Credit)
    *   Income Tax Expense: $1,000 (Debit)

**Consequence of Forgotten Adjustments:**
If an adjusting entry to recognize accrued salaries payable is forgotten, the liability (Salaries Payable) will be understated [source: acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-3].

## Preparing Financial Statements

After the adjusted trial balance is prepared, the next step in the accounting cycle is to prepare the financial statements. The income statement is normally prepared first [source: acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-8]. The adjusted trial balance is used to prepare the income statement [source: acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-5].

The financial statements are prepared in a specific order, as shown below [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-12, acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-13]:

**Step 1: Income Statement**
*   **Purpose:** Identifies all revenue and expense accounts to calculate Net Income.
*   **Formula:** Revenues – Expenses = Net Income.

**General Format of an Income Statement** [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-14]
*   Sales Revenues
*   Less: Cost of Goods Sold
*   **Gross Profit (Gross Margin)**
*   Less: Operating Expenses
*   **Operating Income**
*   Add/Less: Non-operating Income/Expense
*   Add/Less: Interest Income/Interest Expense
*   Add/Less: Other Gain/Loss
*   Less: Income Tax Expense
*   Add/Less: Non-recurring events
*   **Net Income**

**Minion Co. Income Statement for the Year Ended December 31, 2022** [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-15]
| | Amount |
| :-------------------------- | :------ |
| Sales Revenue | $73,500 |
| Less: Cost of Sales | $16,700 |
| **Gross Profit** | **$56,800** |
| **Less Expenses:** | |
| Salaries Expense | $20,000 |
| General Admin Expense | $7,000 |
| Rent Expense | $7,000 |
| Depreciation Expense | $10,000 |
| Bad Debt Expense | $1,800 |
| **Operating Income** | **$11,000** |
| Less: Income Tax Expense | $1,000 |
| **Net Income** | **$10,000** |

**Step 2: Statement of Changes in Equity**
*   **Purpose:** Determines the ending balance of equity, particularly Retained Earnings, using the Net Income from the Income Statement.
*   **Formula:** Beginning Equity + Share Capital Changes + Net Income – Dividends Declared = Ending Equity.
    *   Specifically for Retained Earnings: Beginning Retained Earnings + Net Income – Dividends Declared = Ending Retained Earnings [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-12].

**Minion Co. Statement of Changes in Equity for the Year Ended December 31, 2022** [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-16]
| | Share Capital | Retained Earnings | Total Equity |
| :---------------- | :------------ | :---------------- | :----------- |
| Beginning Balance | $120,000 | $31,000 | $151,000 |
| Net Income | | $10,000 | $10,000 |
| Dividends Declared | | $(4,500) | $(4,500) |
| **Ending Balance** | **$120,000** | **$36,500** | **$156,500** |

**Step 3: Statement of Financial Position (Classified)**
*   **Purpose:** Presents the assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific point in time, using the ending cash balance from the Statement of Cash Flow (covered in Chapter 14) and the ending equity from the Statement of Changes in Equity.
*   **Format:**
    *   **Assets:**
        *   **Current Assets:** To be used, or turned into cash, within a year (e.g., cash, receivables, inventory, prepaid expenses) [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-17].
        *   **Noncurrent Assets:** To be used for more than a year (e.g., long-term investments, fixed assets, intangible assets) [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-17].
    *   **Liabilities:**
        *   **Current Liabilities:** To be resolved within a year (e.g., accounts payable, salaries payable, unearned revenue) [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-17].
        *   **Noncurrent Liabilities:** To be resolved in more than a year (e.g., long-term debt, bonds payable) [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-17].
    *   **Owners' Equity:**
        *   Share Capital
        *   Retained Earnings [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-17]

## Closing the Books

The closing process is the final step in the accounting cycle for a period, where all revenue, expense, and dividend account balances are transferred to Retained Earnings [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-19, acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-21].

**Permanent vs. Temporary Accounts** [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-20]

*   **Real (Permanent) Accounts:**
    *   These are **Balance Sheet accounts**: Assets, Liabilities, and Stockholders' Equity (Share Capital, Retained Earnings).
    *   Balances are **not closed** at the end of the accounting period.
    *   Ending balances are **carried over** to the next accounting period as the beginning balance.

*   **Nominal (Temporary) Accounts:**
    *   These are **Income Statement accounts**: Revenues, Expenses, Gains, and Losses.
    *   **Dividends** are also considered temporary accounts for closing purposes.
    *   Accounts are **closed** at the end of the accounting period to Retained Earnings.
    *   Ending balances are **reset to zero**, so the next accounting period's beginning balance is zero.

**The Closing Process Steps**
1.  **Close all revenue and expense accounts to Retained Earnings.** The net effect of this transfer is the period's net income (or loss) increasing (or decreasing) retained earnings. When closing revenue accounts, you typically debit revenue accounts and credit expense accounts [source: acc1701x-lecture-post-lecture-04-supplement-pdf#section-9].
2.  **Close Dividends to Retained Earnings.** Dividends are not expenses and are not reported on the income statement; they represent distributions of earnings to stockholders [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-21].

**Minion Co. Closing Journal Entries** [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-22]

**1. Close Revenue & Expense Accounts to Retained Earnings:**
| Account | Debit | Credit |
| :---------------------- | :---- | :----- |
| Sales Revenue | $73,500 | |
| Cost of Sales | | $16,700 |
| Salaries Expense | | $20,000 |
| General Admin Expense | | $7,000 |
| Rent Expense | | $7,000 |
| Depreciation Expense | | $10,000 |
| Bad Debt Expense | | $1,800 |
| Income Tax Expense | | $1,000 |
| Retained Earnings | | $10,000 |
*(To close revenue and expense accounts to Retained Earnings. The credit to Retained Earnings represents Net Income.)*

**2. Close Dividends to Retained Earnings:**
| Account | Debit | Credit |
| :---------------- | :---- | :----- |
| Retained Earnings | $4,500 | |
| Dividends Declared | | $4,500 |
*(To close Dividends Declared to Retained Earnings.)*

After the closing process, all nominal (temporary) accounts will have a zero balance [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-23]. Among the options given in the supplement (Unearned Revenue, Dividends Payable, Salaries Expense, Retained Earnings), **Salaries Expense** is the only temporary account that will have a zero balance after closing. Unearned Revenue, Dividends Payable, and Retained Earnings are permanent accounts.

### Post-Closing Trial Balance

After closing entries are posted, a **post-closing trial balance** is prepared. This trial balance contains only permanent accounts (assets, liabilities, and equity) because all temporary accounts have been closed to zero. The retained earnings balance in the post-closing trial balance should match the ending retained earnings balance from the statement of changes in equity.

## Financial Analysis: Profit Margin

**Profit margin** is a profitability ratio that measures how much net profit is generated from each dollar of sales [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-26].
*   It is also known as "Return on Sales."
*   It is useful for assessing a company's operating efficiency in generating profit from its sales activity.
*   A high profit margin indicates potential for future growth.

**Formula:**
Profit Margin = Net Profit / Net Sales [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-26]

### Example: ComfortDelGro and Uber Comparison

Analyzing ComfortDelGro's profit margin over three years [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-27, acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-28]:
| | 2021 | 2020 | 2019 |
| :---------------------- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Net Profit ($'mil) | 160 | 92 | 318 |
| Sales Revenue ($'mil) | 3,538 | 3,243 | 3,901 |
| **Profit Margin** | **4.52%** | **2.83%** | **8.16%** |

ComfortDelGro's profit margin declined from 8.1% in 2019 to 2.8% in 2020 but nearly doubled to 4.5% in 2021.

Comparing ComfortDelGro with Uber reveals a stark contrast [source: acc1701x-lecture-lecture-04-slides-completing-accounting-cycle-pdf#section-28]:
| | 2021 | 2020 | 2019 |
| :---------------------- | :------ | :------ | :------ |
| Net Profit ($'mil) | -8,506 | -6,768 | -8,506 |
| Sales Revenue ($'mil) | 17,455 | 11,139 | 13,000 |
| **Profit Margin** | **-48.73%** | **-60.76%** | **-65.43%** |

Uber has shown a consistently negative profit margin over these three years, indicating unprofitability. This raises questions about whether a negative profit margin can still signal growth for specific types of companies and their long-term viability.
