# ES2660

ES2660 is a Year 1 Semester 1 module designed to develop students' abilities to critically question, analyze, and evaluate socio-economic issues in information technology. The course aims to equip students with skills to articulate their analysis effectively in both written and oral forms [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-course-overview-and-assessments-7oct-pdf#section-1].

The module emphasizes developing critical thinking skills, drawing on models by Ennis (2015) and Paul and Elder (2020), and fostering critical digital literacy using Hinrichsen & Coombs' (2013) approach. This balances technical IT skills with human capacities such as creativity and communication [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-course-overview-and-assessments-7oct-pdf#section-1].

## Key Themes and Topics

### Critical Thinking

The course introduces core concepts and practices of critical thinking. It defines critical thinking as self-directed, self-disciplined, self-monitored, and self-corrective thinking, essential for improving thought quality [source:es2660-the-miniature-guide-to-critical-thinking-concepts-and-tools-pdf#section-1]. Robert Ennis's framework further defines it as "reasonable reflective thinking focused on deciding what to believe or do" [source:es2660-sg16-tohpinren-cr-docx#section-1]. Students learn to utilize a critical thinking "mental tool box" for analysis, discussion, and writing [source:es2660-t1-critical-thinking-glossary-pdf#section-1].

The module explicitly references frameworks such as [[ennis-critical-thinking-framework|Ennis's Framework of Critical Thinking]] and [[paul-elder-critical-thinking-framework|Paul & Elder's Critical Thinking Model]] to provide structured approaches to critical analysis [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-course-overview-and-assessments-7oct-pdf#section-1]. A [[critical-thinking-glossary|glossary of critical thinking terms]] serves as a foundational resource [source:es2660-t1-critical-thinking-glossary-pdf#section-1].

### Communicating in the Information Age

This segment focuses on developing critical digital literacy and effective communication strategies in contemporary contexts. The course roadmap covers understanding digital texts and critical thinking (Weeks 2-4), engaging in academic conversations (Weeks 7-8), and producing written digital text (Weeks 9-12) [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-slides-t03-ca1-a-complete-guide-pdf#section-1].

*   **Online Information Consumption (CA1)**: Students prepare for CA1 through a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for critically consuming online information [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-slides-t03-ca1-a-complete-guide-pdf#section-1].
*   **Academic Conversations & Panel Discussions**: These activities aim to build content knowledge, develop academic conversation skills, foster intellectual curiosity, and apply questioning skills. Panel discussions specifically prepare students for the CA2 online commentary [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-slides-t12-panels-1-2-prompts-pdf#section-1]. Good practices include providing relevant examples, citing credible sources, and defining key terms [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-slides-t14-updated-pdf#section-1].
*   **Online Commentary (CA2)**: This assessment involves producing written digital text, often informed by content derived from panel discussions and critical analysis of socio-economic issues [source:es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-slides-t14-updated-pdf#section-1, es2660-ay2223-s1-aug2022-slides-t12-panels-1-2-prompts-pdf#section-1].
*   **EdTech and Educational Inequality**: A significant socio-economic issue explored is the impact of EdTech on educational inequality. Arguments are presented that EdTech, despite its potential for accessibility, may exacerbate the gap between the rich and poor by creating new barriers related to technology access and conducive learning environments [source:es2660-ca2-sg16-tohpinren-docx#section-1, es2660-draft1-docx#section-1].
