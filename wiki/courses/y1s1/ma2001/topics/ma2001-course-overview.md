# MA2001 Course Overview

MA2001 Linear Algebra I is a core module offered at NUS, with its content broadly applicable across various disciplines [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-1]. Previously, before 2021, the module was known as MA1101R, and archives can be found under this former code [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-1]. This module serves as a first course in linear algebra, introducing fundamental concepts within the context of Euclidean spaces Rⁿ [source:ma2001-ma2001-trial-run-2022-pdf#section-1]. Students are expected to develop computational skills and geometric intuition related to vectors and matrices, alongside understanding proofs of results in this concrete setting. Some applications of linear algebra are also presented [source:ma2001-ma2001-trial-run-2022-pdf#section-1].

## Blended Learning Approach

The MA2001 module employs a blended learning strategy, combining recorded video lectures with weekly live sessions [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-2, ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-3].

### Recorded Lectures
The primary component of the lecture material consists of recorded videos that cover the textbook section by section [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-2]. Students are required to complete a [weekly online quiz](/ma2001-exam-information#weekly-online-quizzes) after viewing these videos [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-2].

### Live Lectures
Weekly live lectures are held to review previous topics, provide additional examples, and offer a preview of the current week's topics [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-3]. There are two lecture groups, and students only need to register for one:

*   **Group 1**: Mondays, 2:00 PM - 4:30 PM at LT27, lectured by A/Prof Ma Siu Lun [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-4, ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-5].
*   **Group 2**: Tuesdays, 10:00 AM - 12:30 PM at LT27, lectured by A/Prof Dilip Raghavan [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-4, ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-5].

Each live lecture session is 1.5 hours long [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-4].

*   **Authors**: S.L. Ma, K.L. Ng and V. Tan [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-7]
*   **Publisher**: McGraw Hill, 2016 [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-7]

Students are strongly advised to obtain a copy. An online version is available through LINC: https://linc.nus.edu.sg/record=b4220761 [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-7].

## Class Components and Assessment

The module comprises various learning and assessment components:

### Learning Components
*   **Recorded/Live Lectures**: Weeks 1 to 13 [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-10].
*   **Tutorials**: Weekly from Weeks 3 to 13 [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-10].
*   **Labs**: Held in Weeks 4, 6, 8, and 10 [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-10].

### Assessment Components
| Component                     | Weeks Covered      | Weightage |
| :---------------------------- | :----------------- | :-------- |
| 12 Weekly Online Quizzes      | Weeks 1 to 12      | 15%       |
| 4 Homeworks                   | Weeks 5, 7, 10, 12 | 20%       |
| 1 Lab Assignment              | Week 13            | 5%        |

#### Weekly Online Quizzes
There are 12 graded online quizzes on LumiNUS from Week 1 to Week 12, based on the corresponding video lectures [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-11]. Quizzes open Monday 00:00 and close Friday 18:00 of each week (except Week 1 quiz, which shares the deadline with Week 2 for late enrolments) [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-11]. Each quiz can be attempted twice, and the best score is taken [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-11].

#### Tutorial Classes
Tutorials start from Week 3 and are held weekly [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-12]. Tutors discuss questions, mainly from the textbook. Students should download questions from LumiNUS and attempt solutions before class. Solutions are uploaded to LumiNUS the week after [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-12].

#### Homeworks
There are 4 graded homework assignments. Questions are available on LumiNUS, and answer scripts must be submitted as PDF files via LumiNUS by the deadline. Late submissions will be penalized [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-13].

#### MATLAB and Computing Laboratories
[MATLAB](/matlab-basics) is used for laboratory sessions [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-14]. NUS students can install MATLAB on their personal computers using their NUSNET ID (instructions available at https://sm05.stf.nus.edu.sg/studentmatlab/) [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-14].

There are 4 optional laboratory sessions in Weeks 4, 6, 8, and 10 [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-15]. Students should download worksheets from LumiNUS, solve them using MATLAB, and attend sessions if guidance is needed. Complete answers are uploaded to LumiNUS the following week [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-15].

#### Lab Assignment
After completing the 4 laboratory sessions, a graded lab assignment is due in Week 13 [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-16]. The worksheet is available on LumiNUS, and the MATLAB-assisted answer script must be submitted as a PDF via LumiNUS before the deadline. Late submissions are penalized [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-16].

## How to Study Mathematics at this Level

Studying mathematics at this level differs significantly from secondary school, where the focus is primarily on problem-solving through examples [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-19, ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-20]. In MA2001, the aim is to build a theory, such as the theory of [vector spaces](/vector-spaces-subspaces-intro) [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-20].

The textbook typically presents topics in the following order: motivation, [definitions](/vector-spaces-subspaces-intro), examples, results and properties (theorems and remarks), followed by more examples [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-18].

To succeed, students must:
1.  **Understand Definitions Thoroughly**: This is crucial as skipping definitions, theorems, and remarks (a common habit from secondary school) will not work here [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-19, ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-20].
2.  **Follow Derivations**: Understand how results and properties are derived from the definitions [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-20]. Examples serve to aid in conceptual understanding, not as the primary learning method [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-20].
3.  **Ask Early**: If any problems arise, especially with the first three chapters, ask the lecturer or post on the forum. Early misunderstandings can negatively impact later topics, as the course builds a theoretical foundation [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-21].
4.  **Revise Topics Before Attempting Questions**: Tutorial and homework assignments often explicitly state which topics to revise. Students should take this advice seriously and understand the underlying theory before attempting questions, as many questions are theory-based and may not have direct examples in the textbook [source:ma2001-lecture-introduction-pdf#section-21].
